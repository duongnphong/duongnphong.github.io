  1. Retrospective Contribution Analysis of Intrinsic 3D Breast Features for Esthetic Outcome Evaluation of Breast Reconstruction Surgery
    Nam Phong Duong, Takumi Sonoi, Yoshihiro Sowa, and Masayuki Fukuzawa
    In Intelligent Systems and Data Science, 2026
    Abs

    In plastic surgery, an objective technique for evaluating the esthetic outcome of reconstructed breasts from breast images is required, because current evaluation relies on visual assessment by plastic surgeons, which is subject to inter-rater bias and variability. Conventional two-dimensional (2D) breast images are not always suitable for examining the esthetic contribution of intrinsic breast features, as they only provide partial appearances of the breast. While, specially-acquired three-dimensional (3D) images reflect those intrinsic features more comprehensively, its application has been hindered by the complexity of 3D geometry and the extensive implementation efforts required for a large number of cases. In this study, we proposed a novel technique to examine the contribution of intrinsic breast features to specific esthetic viewpoint, even with a limited number of cases, by pre-extracting relevant 3D geometric and color features. This approach enabled the analysis of the correlation between all combinations of 3D features and esthetic scores rated by plastic surgeons. Results from 174 clinical cases revealed that 3D geometric and color features exhibited distinct correlation patterns across different viewpoints, suggesting viewpoint-specific esthetic contributions. For each viewpoint, the top 10 correlated features were identified and used as predictors for multiple linear regression (MLR) modeling. The total esthetic score, aggregated from all the viewpoint-specific MLR models trained on high inter-rater agreement cases, demonstrated a strong correlation }}r}}r = 0.89 with the rater average score. These findings support the effectiveness of the proposed technique and its potential for appearance-based esthetic grading using conventional 2D breast images.

  1. Integration of Cobot Technology for Cold Plasma Wound Treatment in the Medical Industry
    Nguyen Chi Hung, Duong Nam Phong, Masayuki Fukuzawa, and Vu Le Huy
    In Proceedings of the 4th Annual International Conference on Material, Machines, and Methods for Sustainable Development (MMMS2024), 2025
    Abs

    The application of collaborative robots (cobots) in the medical industry has been limited due to the nature of human service professions, which require specialized care and professional skills. This article presents a method for using cobots to support the treatment of open wounds using cold plasma, offering a sterilization alternative to conventional antiseptic solutions and bandages. The proposed method involves precise control of the cold plasma tip, maintaining a very low moving speed (approximately 0.5 mm/s) about 5 mm away from the wound surface. Although this operation demands significant time, patience, and technique, the integration of cobot technology presents a promising solution to minimize labor-intensive and expensive repetitive tasks in the medical industry. Specifically, this article discusses the integration of the Cobot Dobot Nova 2 and 3D camera to control the movement of the cold plasma nozzle. The research team has developed a novel control program and interface, leading to a flexible automation solution for medical operations, which has been showcased at domestic exhibitions and is poised for commercialization.
